
\section{Results}

\noindent The empirical validation conducted through the ICUM-SIM environment demonstrates that the proposed decentralized localization framework effectively balances communication overhead with positioning precision.

\subsection*{6.1 Efficiency & Message Reduction (Experiment A)}
\noindent Analysis of the Event-Triggered Messaging (ETM) protocol (Experiment A) reveals a significant reduction in network load. As illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:A_Tx}, the system achieved a 76.17\% decrease in total messages sent compared to the periodic baseline, reducing packet counts from 34,390 to 8,195 over a 600-second interval. It is crucial to note that both the Baseline and ETM configurations utilize the identical distributed spring-relaxation algorithm for coordinate resolution. The performance variance is solely attributable to the \textit{timing} of the ranging inputs (Periodic vs. Reactive), ensuring that the comparison isolates the efficiency of the communication protocol rather than the positioning mathematics.

\noindent Despite this reduction in transmission frequency, the aligned Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) remained largely consistent with the baseline (see Figure \ref{fig:A_RSME}), indicating that the IMU-controlled logic successfully filters redundant updates without compromising real-time tracking accuracy. Furthermore, this reduction in radio traffic translates directly to energy conservation. Figure \ref{fig:battery} highlights that as the proportion of energy budget dedicated to communication increases, the operational lifetime extension provided by ETM scales exponentially, potentially doubling system longevity in high-traffic scenarios.

\subsection*{6.2 Robustness to Measurement Noise (Experiment B)}
\noindent The system's resilience to sensor inaccuracy was evaluated by subjecting the ranging engine to increasing Gaussian noise levels. Results shown in Figure \ref{fig:B_MAE} indicate that the distributed spring-relaxation algorithm acts as an effective low-pass filter against transient errors. The formation maintained functional relative localization (RMSE $<$ 0.5m) even when UWB ranging variance was increased to $3\sigma$ levels ($\approx 30$cm noise), verifying the stability of the cooperative optimization approach under degraded hardware conditions.

\subsection*{6.3 Scalability & Rigidity (Experiment C)}
\noindent Scalability experiments highlight a positive correlation between node density and localization precision. High-density configurations ($N=100$) achieved the highest accuracy with a final RMSE of approximately 0.09m, benefiting from the redundancy of measurements (Figure \ref{fig:C_convergence_static}). In contrast, intermediate cluster sizes ($N=20-35$) exhibited a "Rigidity Transition," where the network graph remained computationally "floppy" and susceptible to geometric distortion during motion.
\noindent Furthermore, node motion in dense scenarios ($N \ge 50$) was observed to act as a catalyst for convergence (Figure \ref{fig:C_convergence_moving}). This motion-assisted convergence phenomenon allows the network to utilize movement to resolve local minima and "unstuck" distorted geometries, suggesting that mobile swarms are inherently more self-correcting than static ones.

\section*{7. Limitations}

\noindent Despite robust simulation performance, several architectural limitations must be acknowledged.

\noindent \textbf{Traffic Scalability:} The most critical constraint is the quadratic explosion ($O(N^2)$) of traffic during ranging. While the background coordination traffic scales linearly, each ranging poll triggers unicast responses from all reachable neighbors. In simulations with $N > 75$, this saturation began to induce packet loss, suggesting that the current all-to-all ranging protocol requires spatial partitioning or sub-clustering to scale to thousands of nodes.

\noindent \textbf{Simulator Validity:} The ICUM-SIM environment employs two key simplifications: a Gaussian noise model for UWB (ignoring multi-path biases) and a binary ray-casting model for occlusion (ignoring diffraction). Additionally, the battery model is linear, whereas real Lithium-Polymer cells exhibit non-linear voltage drop-offs that could trigger the "death spiral" behavior more abruptly in physical deployments.

\section*{8. Potential Applications}

\noindent The findings of this research have direct utility in scenarios where GPS is denied and static infrastructure is impractical.

\noindent \textbf{Search and Rescue (SAR):} In collapsed buildings or dense forests, rapid deployment is critical. The I.C.U.M. system's "anchor-free" capability allows first responders to simply walk into a site and immediately establish a relative tracking mesh, monitoring the position of team members relative to the squad leader without wasting time setting up beacons.

\noindent \textbf{Subterranean & Mining Operations:} Underground tunnels completely block GNSS signals. By utilizing the proposed relative localization framework, mining personnel and machinery can maintain collision-safe formations. The study's "Rigidity Transition" finding suggests that maintaining a minimum density ($N \ge 50$) is crucial for safety in these linear environments.

\noindent \textbf{Autonomous Drone Swarms:} For UAV swarms operating in jamming environments, the "aligned RMSE" metric demonstrates that the swarm can maintain its geometric shape (formation integrity) using only peer-to-peer UWB, ensuring that drones do not collide even if they cannot determine their global latitude.

\noindent \textbf{Construction Equipment Localization:} Construction sites are typically packed with all kinds of machinery and equipment. Since tools and assets are frequently misplaced in such environments, the proposed relative localization framework can be effectively used to localize and track this equipment.

\section*{9. Future Works}

\noindent To bridge the gap between simulation and real-world deployment, several avenues for future research are identified:

\noindent \textbf{Hardware Validation:} The immediate next step is the porting of the TypeScript-based firmware logic to C++ for deployment on ESP32-UWB hardware. Validating the "Death Spiral" and leader election dynamics on physical testbeds will confirm if the energy savings observed in simulation translate to real-world battery life extension.

\noindent \textbf{3D Volumetric Localization:} The current 2D implementation should be extended to 3D to support drone swarms or multi-floor operations. This requires upgrading the spring-relaxation optimization to operate in $(x, y, z)$ space and integrating barometric pressure sensors to constrain the Z-axis drift.

\noindent \textbf{Hybrid Anchor/Anchor-free Architectures:} While this work focused on anchor-free operation, future iterations could incorporate "opportunistic anchors"—nodes that detect GPS lock (e.g., near a cave entrance) and inject absolute position constraints into the mesh, allowing the relative coordinate frame to anchor itself globally without dedicated infrastructure.

\noindent \textbf{Scalable Routing Primitives:} To address the $O(N^2)$ traffic limitation, future work should explore "k-hop" ranging limits or probabilistic neighbor selection, ensuring that bandwidth usage plateaus rather than explodes as density increases.
